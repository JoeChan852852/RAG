Master the Engineer’s Skim Path with AI

Tutorial Session Overview

This tutorial guides second-year engineering students from the chaotic “wild west” of raw AI prompting toward a disciplined, engineer-like reading workflow using The Three-Pass Method. Students first experience the limitations of unstructured AI use, then learn how to design prompts that mirror systematic technical reading.

Royal Insight:
AI becomes powerful only when it follows a clear intellectual structure. The method comes first; the machine comes second.
💡 Reminder:
Students should not treat an LLM as a magic summarization button. They must guide it through deliberate reading passes.

Target Audience and Platform

  • Target Audience: Second-year engineering students
  • Learning Context: AI-assisted engineering literature review
  • Platform Focus: HKU Ed Discussion and students’ chosen LLM tools, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Copilot
  • Core Method: Keshav’s Three-Pass Method for reading technical papers

Course Learning Outcomes Alignment

Course Learning Outcome Tutorial Integration
CLO 1: Utilize modern AI tools to analyze data, solve problems, and support coding. Students use LLMs to filter, compare, and extract core findings from a 10-paper engineering dataset.
CLO 2: Apply core AI technologies to solve introductory engineering problems. Students design structured prompts that act as cognitive frameworks for literature triage.
CLO 3: Evaluate capabilities, limitations, and ethical risks of AI. Students identify hallucinations, missing nuances, contradictions, and overconfident AI summaries.
Royal Insight:
The session is not merely about using AI faster; it is about using AI with engineering judgment.

Phase 1: The “Wild West” Literature Challenge

In the opening challenge, students receive a zip folder containing 10 simple engineering papers. Three papers share a deeply interconnected core topic, while the remaining seven appear superficially similar through shared buzzwords such as neural networks, sustainable concrete, optimization, or smart systems, but are fundamentally unrelated.

Student Task

  • Use any method: manual reading, raw LLM prompting, copy-paste summarization, keyword searching, or personal judgment.
  • Identify the 3 closely related papers.
  • Pinpoint their core thematic connection.
  • Write a brief comparative summary.
  • Flag outright contradictions between the papers.
  • Upload findings to the HKU Ed Discussion board.
  • Spend 10 minutes reviewing peers’ posts.
💡 Tutor Note:
Expect confusion. Students who paste entire PDFs into an LLM may encounter context-window truncation, invented relationships, or over-smoothed summaries that ignore real contradictions.

Phase 2: The Intervention — The Three-Pass Method

Students are then taught to read like engineers: quickly, structurally, and purposefully. The Three-Pass Method replaces uncontrolled reading with staged evaluation.

Pass I: The Grand Overview

Students do not read the full paper. They inspect only the title, abstract, introduction, section headings, conclusion, and references. Their goal is to answer the Five Cs.

  • Category: What type of engineering work is this? Experimental, theoretical, computational, review, or case study?
  • Context: What prior research surrounds the paper?
  • Contributions: What are the central ideas, methods, systems, or claims?
  • Correctness: Are the baseline assumptions reasonable?
  • Clarity: Is the paper well-articulated and structurally coherent?
Royal Insight:
Pass I is an intellectual filter. Its purpose is not comprehension; its purpose is selection.

Pass II: Understanding the Core

Students read more attentively but skip heavy mathematics, long proofs, and minor technical details. Their attention should be directed toward figures, diagrams, flowcharts, tables, and results.

💡 Reminder:
After Pass II, a student should be able to explain the paper’s core system architecture to a classmate without reading from the paper.

Pass III: The Deep Examination

Only the most relevant papers receive a deep examination. Students reconstruct the work mentally, challenge assumptions, identify missing controls, and ask where the method may fail under real engineering conditions.

Phase 3: Prompt Engineering the Skim Path

Students now convert the Three-Pass Method into an AI prompting framework. The key lesson is that the LLM should not decide the reading strategy. The student designs the strategy, and the LLM executes bounded analytical tasks.

Prompt for Pass I: The Triage

Students paste only the title, abstract, and conclusion into the LLM. This prevents overloading the model and forces the analysis to remain focused on first-pass evaluation. 

[System Prompt]
You are an expert engineering research assistant helping a second-year student triage papers using Keshav's Three-Pass Method.

[User Prompt]
Analyze the attached title, abstract, and conclusion.

Provide a concise evaluation based on the Five Cs:
1. Category
2. Context
3. Contributions
4. Correctness: Are initial assumptions standard?
5. Clarity

Conclude with a one-sentence recommendation:
Does this paper merit a deeper Pass II reading regarding [Insert Target Topic]?

Prompt for Pass II: Contradiction Detection

After students isolate the three truly related papers, they provide the full text of only those papers to the LLM. The task now shifts from broad triage to focused comparison. 

[User Prompt]
I have confirmed these 3 papers are highly related.

I want to look for data contradictions or methodology deviations.

Compare their results sections and figures. Generate a Markdown table contrasting:
- Core System Architecture
- Testing Methodology
- Primary Metric or Result
- Conflicting Claims

Highlight cases where Paper A claims X while Paper B claims Y.
Do not invent contradictions. If evidence is unclear, mark it as "Requires human verification."
Royal Insight:
A strong prompt does not ask the AI to think vaguely. It assigns a role, limits the evidence, defines the comparison, and demands uncertainty labels.

Phase 4: Post-Method Reflection and Class Discussion

Students revisit the original 10-paper set using the structured AI-assisted Three-Pass Method. They update their findings and post a clean comparison table to the HKU Ed Discussion thread.

Discussion Questions

  • How did your Phase 1 blind prompting results differ from your Phase 3 Three-Pass prompting results?
  • Where did the LLM try to smooth over a contradiction that actually required human engineering judgment?
  • Which papers looked similar at first but became clearly unrelated after Pass I?
  • Which details could the LLM extract effectively, and which required human verification?
💡 Reminder:
The goal of reflection is not to prove that AI is useless or perfect. The goal is to understand where AI is useful, where it fails, and where engineering judgment must intervene.

Assessment Evidence

  • Initial HKU Ed Post: Demonstrates how students approach literature without structure.
  • Peer Review Notes: Reveals variation and inconsistency in unstructured AI use.
  • Structured Comparison Table: Shows ability to apply the Three-Pass Method through AI prompts.
  • Reflection Response: Evaluates student understanding of AI limitations, hallucinations, and human oversight.

Final Takeaway

AI will not replace a student’s ability to read engineering literature. Instead, it amplifies the student’s reading strategy. If students feed an LLM a chaotic strategy, they receive chaotic analysis. If they first master the Three-Pass Method and then program the AI to follow that discipline, they gain speed, structure, and sharper critical judgment.

The Engineer’s Principle:
Structure first. Automation second. Judgment always.
